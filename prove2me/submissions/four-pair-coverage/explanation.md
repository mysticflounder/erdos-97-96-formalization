Assume that A ∩ D, B ∩ C, and B ∩ D are all empty. Together with the three given disjointness assumptions, this makes A, B, C, and D pairwise disjoint. Each contains two elements, so their union contains eight elements. But their union is a subset of U, which contains at most seven elements. This is a contradiction.

In the exact-16 geometric argument, A, B, C, and D are the two-point outside supports of four selected rows. Pairwise separation supplies the three disjointness hypotheses. An A/D overlap produces the existing OldB motif; a B/C overlap produces OldC; and a B/D overlap produces NewB. Thus the counting lemma replaces enumeration of all support assignments. The geometric interpretation and motif contradictions are separate results; this submission proves the finite-set lemma only.
