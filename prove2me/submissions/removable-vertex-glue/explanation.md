# Why a removable vertex gives descent

A removable vertex belongs to the point set and can be deleted without losing
the property that every remaining point has four equidistant witnesses. The
smaller set is the original set with that vertex erased.

Membership of the deleted vertex makes the cardinality decrease by one. The
assumption that the original set has at least two points makes the erased set
nonempty. Strict convex position passes to subsets, and removability supplies
the repeated-distance property on the erased set.

This theorem proves the consequence of finding a removable vertex. It does
not prove that every large counterexample has one; that is the separate open
large-cardinality milestone.
