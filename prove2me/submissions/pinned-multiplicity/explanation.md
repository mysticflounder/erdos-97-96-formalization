# Pinned-multiplicity reformulation

For a finite point set `A` and a center `p`, the definition takes the maximum,
over positive distances realized from `p`, of the number of points of `A` at
that distance. The maximum is zero when no positive distance is realized.

For the first implication, a witnessed radius is inserted into the finite
supremum. For the reverse implication, a radius attaining a positive supremum
is selected. The remaining theorems apply these two directions under the
universal quantifiers and specialize the elementary natural-number negation at
four.

The final theorem unfolds the mission's Problem 97 statement and proves both
directions connecting it to the pinned bound. It does not prove either open
statement by itself.

The pinned viewpoint is due to Erdős's formulation of repeated distances from
one vertex. The Lean definitions and two-direction proofs are this project's
own formalization.
