## 2. Radial-order lemma

Target, lines 89-107:

> “In the open interior wedge at `v`, the rays from `v` to the other
> vertices occur in the same order as those vertices on the boundary
> arc from one neighbor of `v` to the other.”

**Certified.** For an interior ray from a vertex of a convex polygon, the
intersection with the polygon is a segment starting at that vertex; hence the
ray has a unique second boundary intersection on the opposite boundary arc.
Strict convexity/no-three-collinear excludes two distinct selected vertices on
one ray. Along the opposite arc the ray direction is therefore continuous and
injective into an angular interval of length less than `pi`, hence monotone.
All intervening ray gaps are positive. This proves precisely the additivity and
strict-positivity facts used later.

No later decomposition applies this lemma across the exterior/reflex sector.

## 3. Exact proof of R2 x merged-column C2

### Claim M1: setup and isosceles identities

Target, lines 111-138:

> “Rename the cyclic order `A, C, B, D, E, P, F` with unit distances
> `AF = AP = BF = CE = DE = DP = 1`.”

and

> “`X < pi/2, Z < pi/2, Y = (pi - V)/2,
> angle EPD = (pi - W)/2,
> angle APF = (pi - U)/2.`”

**Certified.**

- `AF=BF` makes `X=angle FAB` a base angle of nondegenerate isosceles
  triangle `AFB`, so `0<X<pi/2`.
- `CE=DE` makes `Z=angle CDE` a base angle of nondegenerate isosceles
  triangle `CDE`, so `0<Z<pi/2`.
- `AP=DP` gives the base angles
  `angle PAD=angle ADP=(pi-angle APD)/2=(pi-V)/2`.
- `DE=DP` gives
  `angle EPD=(pi-angle PDE)/2=(pi-W)/2`.
- `AF=AP` gives
  `angle APF=(pi-angle FAP)/2=(pi-U)/2`.

The target's equalities and strict inequalities therefore have the right
vertices and the right apex/base assignments.

### Claim M2: the angle decomposition at A stays in the interior sector

Target, lines 140-155:

> “At `A`, radial order inside its polygon angle is
> `F, P, E, D, B, C`.
> Therefore
> `X = angle FAP + angle PAD + angle DAB > U + Y`.”

**Certified.** Restricting the hull cycle
`(A,C,B,D,E,P,F)` at vertex `A`, the opposite boundary arc from neighbor
`F` to neighbor `C` is exactly

`F,P,E,D,B,C`.

Thus `angle FAP=U`; `angle PAD=Y` is itself the sum of the positive
`P-E-D` ray gaps; and the remaining `angle DAB` is positive. All three lie
inside the polygon angle at `A`, whose total is below `pi`. Hence
`X=U+Y+angle DAB>U+Y`. There is no supplementary-angle substitution.

Combining this with `X<pi/2` and `Y=(pi-V)/2` gives exactly

`U+(pi-V)/2 < pi/2`, hence `2U<V`.

### Claim M3: the angle decomposition at D stays in the interior sector

Target, lines 158-173:

> “At `D`, radial order inside its polygon angle is
> `B, C, A, F, P, E`.
> Therefore
> `Z = angle CDA + angle ADP + angle PDE > Y + W`.”

**Certified.** At `D`, the opposite arc from neighbor `B` to neighbor `E`
is exactly

`B,C,A,F,P,E`.

Inside that wedge, `angle CDA>0`; `angle ADP=Y` contains the intermediate
ray `F`; and `angle PDE=W`. Therefore the displayed sum is exact and
`Z>Y+W`. With `Z<pi/2` and `Y=(pi-V)/2`, this gives exactly `2W<V`.

### Claim M4: the angle decomposition at P stays in the interior sector

Target, lines 176-195:

> “At `P` the ray order is `E, D, B, C, A, F`.”

and

> “`angle EPF = angle EPD + angle DPA + angle APF
> = (pi-W)/2 + V + (pi-U)/2 < pi.`”

**Certified.** At `P`, the opposite arc from neighbor `E` to neighbor `F`
is precisely

`E,D,B,C,A,F`.

Thus the polygon interior angle `angle EPF` is partitioned, in order, at
rays `D` and `A`. The middle angle `angle DPA` contains rays `B,C` but is
the same ordinary angle as `V=angle APD`; it is not the supplementary
sector because it lies within the polygon wedge of size less than `pi`.
The isosceles identities from M1 then give the displayed equality.
Strict convexity gives `angle EPF<pi`, so the algebra is

`pi-(U+W)/2+V<pi`,

hence exactly `2V<U+W`.

### Claim M5: the merged-case contradiction

Target, lines 198-202:

> “But (2) and (3) imply `U+W < V`, contradicting (4) (and `V>0`).
> Thus R2 x C2 has no strictly convex realization.”

## 4. Exact proof of R2 x distinct-column C1

### Claim D1: setup, acute base angles, and radial decompositions

Target, lines 206-245:

> “Rename the cyclic order
> `A, C, B, D, E, H, K, F`
> with unit distances
> `AF = AK = BF = CE = DE = DH = 1`.”

and

> “Consequently
> `X = U + Y + angle DAB > U+Y,
> T = angle CDA + Z + W > Z+W`,
> so
> `U+Y < pi/2, W+Z < pi/2.`”

**Certified.** The unit-distance labels agree with S3.
`AF=BF` makes `X=angle FAB` an acute base angle of triangle `AFB`;
`CE=DE` makes `T=angle CDE` an acute base angle of triangle `CDE`.

At `A`, the opposite arc from neighbor `F` to neighbor `C` is

`F,K,H,E,D,B,C`.

Consequently `angle FAB` is the ordered sum of `angle FAK=U`,
`angle KAD=Y` (which contains rays `H,E`), and the positive
`angle DAB`.

At `D`, the opposite arc from neighbor `B` to neighbor `E` is

`B,C,A,F,K,H,E`.

Consequently `angle CDE` is the ordered sum of the positive
`angle CDA`, `angle ADH=Z` (which contains rays `F,K`), and
`angle HDE=W`.

Every term lies in the relevant interior wedge. Thus
`X>U+Y`, `T>Z+W`, and the two displayed strict upper bounds follow.

### Claim D2: inherited quadrilateral order and angle sum

Target, lines 247-258:

> “The four vertices `A,D,H,K` inherit the strictly convex cyclic order
> `A,D,H,K`.”

and

> “`Y + Z + Q + R = 2pi.`”

**Certified.** Restricting
`(A,C,B,D,E,H,K,F)` to `{A,D,H,K}` gives exactly
`(A,D,H,K)`. Every selected vertex remains extreme and no three become
collinear, so this is a strictly convex quadrilateral. Its interior angles
are, in order,

- at `A`: `angle KAD=Y`;
- at `D`: `angle ADH=Z`;
- at `H`: `angle DHK=Q`;
- at `K`: `angle HKA=R`.

The quadrilateral angle sum is therefore exactly the displayed equation.

### Claim D3: the bound on Q

Target, lines 260-270:

> “Since `DH=DE`, the base angle `angle EHD=(pi-W)/2`. At polygon
> vertex `H`,
> `angle EHK = angle EHD + angle DHK < pi`,
> hence `Q < (pi+W)/2`.”

**Certified.** In triangle `DHE`, `DH=DE` and the apex angle at `D` is
`W=angle HDE`, so the base angle at `H` is `(pi-W)/2`.

At polygon vertex `H`, the interior radial order is

`E,D,B,C,A,F,K`.

Thus the interior angle `angle EHK` is exactly
`angle EHD+angle DHK`; the latter term contains rays `B,C,A,F` and is
the quadrilateral interior angle `Q`. Strict convexity gives
`angle EHK<pi`, hence

`(pi-W)/2+Q<pi`,

or exactly `Q<(pi+W)/2`.

### Claim D4: the bound on R

Target, lines 273-283:

> “Similarly `AF=AK` gives `angle AKF=(pi-U)/2`, and at polygon vertex
> `K`,
> `angle HKF = angle HKA + angle AKF < pi`,
> hence `R < (pi+U)/2`.”

**Certified.** In triangle `FAK`, `AF=AK` and the apex angle at `A` is
`U=angle FAK`, so `angle AKF=(pi-U)/2`.

At polygon vertex `K`, the interior radial order is

`H,E,D,B,C,A,F`.

Thus `angle HKF` is exactly `angle HKA+angle AKF`; the first term
contains rays `E,D,B,C` and is the quadrilateral interior angle `R`.
Again the sum is the polygon interior rather than the exterior sector.
Since it is below `pi`, the claimed bound follows.

### Claim D5: the distinct-case contradiction

Target, lines 286-300:

> “Equations (6)--(8) imply
> `Y+Z > pi - (U+W)/2`.”

and

> “But (5) implies
> `Y+Z < pi - (U+W)`.”

**Certified.** From the quadrilateral sum and the strict bounds on `Q,R`,

`Y+Z = 2pi-Q-R
       > 2pi-(pi+W)/2-(pi+U)/2
       = pi-(U+W)/2`.

From `U+Y<pi/2` and `W+Z<pi/2`,

`Y+Z<pi-(U+W)`.

The angles `U,W` are strictly positive, so
`pi-(U+W) < pi-(U+W)/2`. The upper bound is therefore strictly below
the lower bound. The contradiction is exact.

## 5. Scaling and transposed orientation

### Claim T1: normalization to unit distance loses no generality

Target, lines 28-29:

> “All distances below are normalized to 1. General common distance
> `delta > 0` follows by similarity.”

**Certified.** Scaling every coordinate by `1/delta` preserves vertex
distinctness, strict convexity, cyclic/radial order, and all angles, while
turning every required distance `delta` into `1`. Conversely a unit witness
scales to a `delta` witness. Since distinct polygon vertices cannot have
distance zero, the `delta>0` restriction is exact.

### Claim T2: orientation 2 follows by transposition

Target, lines 5-9:

> “Orientation 2 follows by the already-proved C2 transposition symmetry
> in `../fr-pattern-p3-proof-draft.md`, Proposition 5.”

and target, lines 17-20:

> “therefore the P3/R2 family has no strictly convex Euclidean witness,
> for either column subcase and either transposed orientation.”

**Certified.** Under C2 an original reading has undirected hull cycle

`(a1,...,a_alpha,b_beta,...,b1)`.

After swapping row and column sides, the target C2 cycle is

`(b1,...,b_beta,a_alpha,...,a1)`,

which is exactly the reversal of the original undirected cycle. Therefore the
same polygon realizes the transposed matrix under the same convention.
Transposition exchanges the two printed lettered P3 orientations and preserves
the six underlying cross-cut distances. This is Proposition 5 and its
corollary in `fr-pattern-p3-proof-draft.md`, lines 279-308.

The result proved here for orientation 1 therefore transfers subcase by
subcase to orientation 2. No single-axis reversal, which would be invalid
under C2, is being used.
